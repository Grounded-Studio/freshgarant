How long does homemade soup last in the fridge?

Short answer: Homemade soup keeps 2 days in the fridge, according to the Voedingscentrum, and about 3 months in the freezer. Cool it quickly, get it into the fridge within 2 hours and reheat it until steaming hot, only once. Unopened shop-bought soup can often be eaten up to 2 months past its best-before date.

How long is homemade soup good?

The Voedingscentrum gives 2 days in the fridge for homemade soup, whether it is a thick soup with meat and vegetables or a clear chicken soup. The FSA's rule for leftovers matches: eat them within 48 hours or freeze them. That makes soup one of the best dishes to cook in bulk, as long as you freeze what you will not eat within two days.

Cool a big pan of soup quickly

A large pan of soup holds its heat for hours, and bacteria can grow while it slowly cools. The FSA calls the range between 8 and 63°C the danger zone. Both sources advise cooling food quickly and getting it into the fridge within two hours.

  • Divide it into smaller containers. Small portions cool much faster than one big pan.
  • Use cold water. The Voedingscentrum's tip: place the pan or container in a layer of cold water.
  • Do not put a hot pan straight into the fridge; the FSA advises cooling cooked food first, then refrigerating it within one to two hours.

Can you freeze soup?

Yes, soup freezes very well: the Voedingscentrum gives about 3 months. Freeze it in portions, in containers or freezer bags laid flat, and leave some room because liquid expands. Write the name and date on it. Soups with pasta or potato pieces can turn a bit soft; you can also freeze the soup base and add those when reheating.

Reheating soup

Heat soup until it is steaming hot all the way through, stirring well. The Voedingscentrum advises against reheating leftovers a second time, because each time the food passes through room temperature, bacteria get another chance to grow. So only heat up what you will eat now, and leave the rest in the fridge or freezer.

Shop-bought soup

For unopened soup in a pouch, tin or carton, the Voedingscentrum says it can often be eaten up to 2 months after its best-before date, and possibly longer, if it still looks fine and the packaging is intact. Bulging tins or pouches go in the bin. Once opened, shop-bought soup counts as a leftover: 2 days in the fridge. Fresh chilled soups usually carry a use-by date; then the date decides.

How to tell if soup has gone off

A sour smell, bubbles or fizzing in soup that is not boiling, or mould mean the soup has spoiled. But bacteria that cause food poisoning cannot always be smelled, so go by time: more than 2 days in the fridge means it goes.

Tips to waste less with soup

  • Soup is the perfect home for vegetables that are past their best.
  • Freeze single portions for easy lunches.
  • Log soup in FreshGarant when you put it in the fridge or freezer, so you know how long it has been there.

Frequently asked questions

How long does homemade soup last in the fridge?

2 days, according to the Voedingscentrum; the FSA's rule for leftovers is 48 hours.

Can you freeze soup?

Yes, for about 3 months. Freeze it in portions and leave room for it to expand.

How do you cool soup quickly?

Divide it into smaller containers and place the pan or containers in cold water. Get it into the fridge within 2 hours.

Can you reheat soup more than once?

Better not. Heat only what you will eat, until steaming hot.

Can you eat shop-bought soup past the date?

Unopened soup with a best-before date often for up to 2 months if the pack is intact. Fresh soup with a use-by date: no.
