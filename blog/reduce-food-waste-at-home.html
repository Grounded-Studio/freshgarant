10 Ways to Reduce Food Waste at Home

Published 26 October 2026 · 2 min read

In the Netherlands every person throws away about 34 kilos of food a year, according to the Voedingscentrum. Most of it is food that simply expired unnoticed. These ten habits make the biggest difference.

Before you shop

  1. Check what you already have. Most double purchases happen because nobody looked in the fridge first.
  2. Plan a few meals. You don't need a full week — three planned dinners already cut impulse buys.
  3. Make a list, and stick to it. Especially for fresh products with a short life.

At home

  1. First in, first out. Put new groceries at the back, older ones at the front.
  2. Know the difference between best before and use by. Best before food is often fine after the date; use by food isn't. Read the difference.
  3. Store food in the right place. Some things last longer outside the fridge, others only inside it. See the fridge chart.
  4. Freeze in time. Bread, meat and leftovers can almost all go in the freezer before they expire. What you can freeze.

In the kitchen

  1. Cook with what needs to go first. Build tonight's meal around the item that expires soonest.
  2. Make a "use it up" meal once a week — soup, stir-fry, omelette or pasta take almost anything.
  3. Cook smaller portions, or plan leftovers. Leftovers keep about two days in the fridge, or months in the freezer.

These are general guidelines for food stored at the right temperature. Always follow the instructions on the packaging, and when in doubt, throw it out. Sources: Voedingscentrum (Netherlands Nutrition Centre) and the EU rules on date marking.
