What Food Can You Freeze, and for How Long?

Published 26 October 2026 · 2 min read

Your freezer is the easiest way to stop throwing food away. Almost anything can go in — if you know how long it keeps and how to thaw it.

At −18 °C bacteria stop growing, so frozen food stays safe for a long time. The times below are about quality: after that, food is still safe but can get dry or lose flavour ("freezer burn").

How long food keeps in the freezer

ProductIn the freezer
Bread and rollsup to 3 months
Minced meat, sausagesup to 3 months
Chicken and other poultryup to 6 months
Beef and pork (lean)6–9 months
Fatty fish (salmon, mackerel)2–3 months
Lean fish (cod, pollock)up to 6 months
Cooked meals, soups, sauces2–3 months
Butterup to 6 months
Grated hard cheeseup to 3 months
Vegetables (blanched)8–12 months
Fruit (berries, banana slices)8–12 months

What you'd better not freeze

  • Lettuce, cucumber, radish: they turn limp and watery.
  • Raw eggs in their shell: they can crack. Beat them first.
  • Mayonnaise and creamy sauces: they separate.
  • Fizzy drinks: the can or bottle can burst.

Freezing tips

  1. Freeze before the use-by date — freezing pauses the clock, it doesn't turn it back.
  2. Portion it: freeze in the amounts you'll use, so you never thaw more than you need.
  3. Squeeze out the air and use freezer bags or boxes against freezer burn.
  4. Write the date on it. Two months later nobody remembers.
  5. Cool cooked food first, then freeze it.

Thawing safely

  • In the fridge is safest: overnight for most products.
  • In the microwave on the defrost setting, and cook it straight away.
  • Not on the counter for meat, fish or poultry — the outside warms up while the inside is still frozen.
  • Eat thawed food within a day, and don't refreeze raw food once thawed. Once you've cooked it, you can freeze the cooked dish again.

These are general guidelines for food stored at the right temperature. Always follow the instructions on the packaging, and when in doubt, throw it out. Sources: Voedingscentrum (Netherlands Nutrition Centre) and the EU rules on date marking.
