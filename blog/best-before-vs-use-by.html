Best Before vs Use By: What's the Difference?

Published 26 October 2026 · 2 min read

Two dates, two very different meanings. Mixing them up is one of the biggest reasons good food ends up in the bin — and occasionally why bad food doesn't.

Best before: about quality

A best before date tells you until when the manufacturer guarantees the product keeps its full quality — taste, texture, colour — when stored as instructed. After that date the product is usually still perfectly safe to eat. It may just be a little less crisp, crunchy or flavourful.

You'll find it on products that don't spoil quickly: pasta, rice, cereal, crisps, tins, jars, chocolate, flour, frozen food and also eggs.

Past the best before date? Check it yourself

  • Look: any mould, discolouration or a bulging lid or tin? Throw it away.
  • Smell: does it smell sour, musty or off? Don't eat it.
  • Taste: a small bite — if it tastes normal, it's fine.

Dry products like pasta or rice can often last months past the date if the pack is closed and dry.

Use by: about safety

A use by date is a safety limit. It's used for perishable food where harmful bacteria can grow even when the product still looks and smells fine: fresh meat, minced meat, poultry, fish, ready meals, fresh dairy and pre-cut salads.

Don't eat a product after its use by date, even if it looks good. Bacteria such as Listeria or Salmonella don't always change the smell or taste.

Save it before the date

Many use-by products can go into the freezer before the date. Freezing pauses the clock: thaw it in the fridge and eat it within a day.

Opened is a different story

Both dates only apply to unopened packs stored as the label says. Once opened, the clock speeds up. The label often says "once opened, consume within 3 days" — that rule beats the printed date.
