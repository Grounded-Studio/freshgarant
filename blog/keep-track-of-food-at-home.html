How to Keep Track of the Food You Have at Home

Published 5 November 2026 · 2 min read

Most households don't waste food because they buy too much. They waste it because they forget what's there: the yoghurt behind the jam, the mince at the bottom of the freezer, the second bag of rice nobody knew about.

Why we lose track

  • Out of sight, out of mind. What's at the back of the fridge or the bottom of the freezer simply stops existing.
  • Three places, no overview. Fridge, freezer and pantry each have their own pile, and nobody has the full picture.
  • Several people shop. One person buys milk, the other buys milk too.

A simple system that works

1. Fixed zones

Give every type of food its own spot: dairy on one shelf, meat and fish on the bottom shelf, an "eat first" box at eye level. In the freezer, use one drawer per type.

2. New at the back, old at the front

When you unpack groceries, move older products forward. It takes ten seconds and stops most surprises.

3. Label the freezer

Write the product and the date on every bag. "Some kind of sauce, probably March" is how freezer food gets thrown away.

4. One list for all three places

The real fix is a single overview of everything you have — fridge, freezer and pantry together — that you can check from the supermarket aisle.

Getting it in fast

A list only works if adding to it is quicker than forgetting. The + menu has quick add buttons for everyday products (milk, bread, eggs…), and you can scan a receipt, a barcode or one photo of your fridge to add several products at once.
