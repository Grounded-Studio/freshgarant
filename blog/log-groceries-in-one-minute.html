4 Ways to Log Your Groceries in Under a Minute

Published 5 November 2026 · 2 min read

A food tracker only helps if your food is actually in it. And nobody wants to type in twenty products after a big shop. Here are four ways to get your groceries in fast, and when to use which.

1. Scan the receipt — best after a big shop

Take a photo of your receipt and FreshGarant reads the product lines and suggests a category, storage place and expiry date for each one. You check the list, fix a name here and there, tap add, done.

Use it when: you come home with a full bag from one supermarket.

2. One photo of your fridge — best for a fresh start

Open the fridge, take one photo, and AI recognises the products on it. Ideal the first time you use the app, or after a holiday when you have no idea what's in there anymore. You can adjust names, amounts and dates before adding them.

Use it when: you want to get everything that's already at home into the app at once.

3. Scan a barcode — best for single products

Point the camera at the barcode and the product is found instantly. Precise, but one at a time.

Use it when: you're adding a few packaged products, or the receipt is long gone.

4. Quick add — best for everyday basics

Milk, bread, eggs, yoghurt… the things you buy every week are one tap away in the + menu. Tap the chip and it's in your stock with a sensible expiry date. No typing, no scanning.

Use it when: you picked up the daily basics on your way home.

Which one is fastest?

SituationFastest method
Weekly shop, 15+ productsReceipt scan
First time using the appFridge photo
2–3 packaged productsBarcode

Bonus: dinner and stock in one go

The new What's for dinner? feature also recognises the products on your fridge photo. While it comes up with three dinner ideas, you can put everything it saw into your stock with one tap — two jobs, one photo.

These are general guidelines for food stored at the right temperature. Always follow the instructions on the packaging, and when in doubt, throw it out. Sources: Voedingscentrum (Netherlands Nutrition Centre) and the EU rules on date marking.
